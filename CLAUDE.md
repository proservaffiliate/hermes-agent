# Content Operator Manual — Replacement-Proof Procedures

Every rule is trigger → action. Run them literally. No interpretation allowed.

---

## 1. Topic Selection

- When you sit down to pick a topic, open the last 30 days of performance data first. Pick only from: (a) a top-5 performer you can extend, (b) a question asked 2+ times by the audience this month, or (c) a documented pain point from a sales call or DM. If none exist, pull the oldest item from the idea backlog. Never invent a topic from imagination.
- When a topic idea contains no named audience problem, rewrite the topic as "How [audience] can [outcome] without [obstacle]." If you can't fill all three slots from real data, kill the topic.
- When two topics tie, choose the one closer to a paid offer. Log the loser to the backlog with today's date.

**Worked example:** Draft topic: "My thoughts on AI trends." No named problem, no audience, no obstacle. Rewrite attempt fails — no data source. Killed. Replaced with backlog item "How solo founders can qualify leads without a sales team" (asked 3x in DMs this month).

**Failure prevented:** Publishing self-indulgent content nobody asked for — the #1 cause of engagement decay.

---

## 2. Research & Claims

- When you write a number, statistic, date, or named-person claim, paste the source URL in a comment on that exact line before writing the next sentence. No URL = delete the claim or rewrite it as opinion ("in my experience...").
- When a source is older than 18 months, search for a newer one. If none exists, add "(as of [source year])" in the published text.
- When a claim comes from a screenshot, thread, or secondhand post, trace it to the original. If the original can't be found in 5 minutes, cut the claim.
- When you quote anyone, copy the quote verbatim from the source and link it. Never reconstruct a quote from memory.

**Worked example:** Draft says "90% of startups fail." 5-minute trace finds no primary source — the figure circulates without origin. Line replaced with "most startups fail (the commonly cited figures are disputed)" and the argument restructured to not depend on the number.

**Failure prevented:** A fabricated statistic going viral with your name on it, torching credibility with the exact audience you sell to.

---

## 3. Hooks & Headlines

- When a draft is finished, write 5 hook variants before choosing one. Each variant must contain either a number, a named outcome, a named enemy (mistake/myth), or a timeframe. Variants missing all four get deleted, not fixed.
- When the hook promises something, find the sentence in the body that delivers it and paste the hook next to it. If no sentence delivers it, change the hook — never pad the body to justify a hook.
- When the hook exceeds 12 words (social) or 60 characters (email subject), cut words until it fits. Cut adjectives first, then qualifiers.

**Worked example:** Chosen hook: "This will change how you think about lead gen." Body check: no sentence delivers a changed mental model — it's a 5-step checklist. Hook replaced with "5 lead-gen steps that book calls without ads" (number + outcome + enemy).

**Failure prevented:** Clickbait gap — hook writes a check the body can't cash, training the audience to ignore you.

---

## 4. Drafting

- When you start a draft, write the CTA and the single takeaway sentence first, at the top of the file. Every section you then write must feed one or the other; when a section feeds neither, cut it.
- When a paragraph passes 4 lines on mobile preview, split it or cut it.
- When you write "very," "really," "actually," "in order to," "I think," or "it's important to note," delete the phrase on sight.
- When you explain a concept, follow it with a concrete example or number within 2 sentences. Concept with no example within 2 sentences = cut the concept or add the example.

**Worked example:** Post about invoice chasing includes a 6-line paragraph on the history of accounts receivable. Takeaway sentence at top of file: "Send the first reminder at day 3, not day 30." History paragraph feeds neither takeaway nor CTA. Cut. Post shortens by 40% and the actionable step moves above the fold.

**Failure prevented:** Bloated posts where the one useful idea drowns — readers skim, get nothing, don't return.

---

## 5. Edit & Fact-Check Pass

- When the draft is "done," close it for a minimum of 2 hours (or overnight for long-form) before editing. Edit in a different format than you wrote it (phone if written on desktop, print/read-aloud for long-form).
- When you edit, run exactly three passes in order — never combined: (1) cut pass: remove 10% of word count minimum; (2) claim pass: click every source comment from Section 2 and confirm the page still says what you cited; (3) read-aloud pass: any sentence you stumble on gets rewritten shorter.
- When the cut pass can't find 10%, cut the weakest full section instead.

**Worked example:** Claim pass clicks a source comment for "pricing page tests lifted conversion 23%" — the linked case study says 2.3%. Typo caught, corrected before publish.

**Failure prevented:** Publishing your own typo as a 10x-inflated claim, then issuing a correction that outperforms the post.

---

## 6. Platform Formatting & Repurposing

- When repurposing one piece across platforms, rewrite the first 2 lines and the CTA natively for each platform. Body may carry over; hook and CTA never carry over verbatim.
- When posting to a platform, check its current link policy before including a link. If links suppress reach on that platform, move the link to the first comment or profile and say so in the post.
- When formatting for mobile, insert a line break after every 1–2 sentences and preview on an actual phone before scheduling.
- When a piece includes an image, write the alt text and check the image legibly renders at thumbnail size. Text in image unreadable at thumbnail = remake the image with fewer than 12 words on it.

**Worked example:** LinkedIn post copied verbatim to X. First line is 240 characters — truncated mid-sentence in the feed with no hook visible. Procedure catches it at the "rewrite first 2 lines natively" step; X version gets a 9-word hook line.

**Failure prevented:** Cross-posting artifacts (truncated hooks, dead formatting, suppressed links) that signal laziness and kill reach.

---

## 7. Offers, CTAs & Compliance

- When a post ends, it gets exactly one CTA. Two candidate CTAs = keep the one closer to revenue, delete the other.
- When content mentions income, results, or client outcomes, attach the real number, the real timeframe, and the words "results not typical" or an equivalent disclaimer. No real number available = no results claim, full stop.
- When content promotes an affiliate product or paid partnership, insert the disclosure ("affiliate link" / "paid partnership") in the same post, above the fold — not in a bio, not in a reply.
- When you recommend a product you haven't personally used in the last 90 days, either use it before publishing or state plainly that you haven't.

**Worked example:** Draft CTA: "Grab the template — link in bio (affiliate)." Disclosure check passes, but a line above reads "students are making $10k/month." No documented student result on file matches that figure. Line cut; replaced with a real documented case: "one client booked 4 calls in week one."

**Failure prevented:** FTC exposure and the trust collapse that follows one screenshot of an unbacked income claim.

---

## 8. Publishing & Scheduling QC

- When scheduling, open the platform's native preview and read the post there — never approve from the drafting tool's view.
- When the post contains a link, click it from the preview. Broken, misdirected, or missing UTM = fix before scheduling.
- When scheduling time-sensitive content, set the timezone explicitly on the scheduler and confirm the rendered local time matches intent.
- When a post goes live, check it within 15 minutes for rendering breaks and reply to comments within the first hour window.

**Worked example:** Preview click on "book a call" link opens last month's expired calendar link. Caught at the link-click step; replaced with the live booking URL and a UTM tag before the post scheduled.

**Failure prevented:** A high-performing post driving traffic to a dead link — the most expensive silent failure in the pipeline.

---

## 9. Performance Review & Iteration

- When 7 days pass after publish, log the piece's numbers (reach, engagement, clicks, replies, conversions) in the tracking sheet the same day. No exceptions for "small" posts.
- When a piece lands in the top 20% of the trailing 30 days, add its topic + hook pattern to the "extend" list within the same logging session.
- When a piece lands in the bottom 20% twice in a row for the same format or topic, retire that format/topic from the rotation for 60 days.
- When you change anything based on data, change one variable at a time (hook style, format, time, CTA) and note which variable in the log.

**Worked example:** Two consecutive carousel posts land bottom 20%. Instinct says "post a third, algorithms fluctuate." Procedure says retire the format 60 days. Log shows the same carousel topic underperformed in text form too — topic retired, not just format, saving three more dead posts.

**Failure prevented:** Repeating what feels good instead of what works — the slow-bleed failure no single post reveals.

---

## FINAL GATE — Run on every piece before sending

1. ☐ Topic traces to a data source (performance, question, or pain point) — not imagination.
2. ☐ Every number/quote/named claim has a live, clicked source; opinions are labeled as opinions.
3. ☐ Hook is delivered by a specific sentence in the body.
4. ☐ One CTA. One takeaway. Every section feeds one of them.
5. ☐ Three edit passes completed in order (cut / claim / read-aloud).
6. ☐ Native preview checked on the actual platform, on a phone.
7. ☐ Every link clicked from the preview and lands correctly.
8. ☐ Results claims have real numbers + disclaimer; affiliate/paid disclosures are above the fold.
9. ☐ Post is scheduled in the confirmed timezone; 15-minute post-publish check is on the calendar.

**If any item fails: fix it and re-run the gate from item 1. Never send anyway.**
